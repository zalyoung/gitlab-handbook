## Summary

The GitLab Contributors Program supports and recognizes both code and non-code contributors to GitLab. This program is owned by the [Contributor Success team](/handbook/marketing/developer-relations/contributor-success/) and guides the contributor journey start to finish. External outreach and growth strategies funnel new contributors into the program on the [Contributors platform](contributors.gitlab.com) where they are guided to onboard, contribute, and level up within our community.

## Objectives

1. Grow the wider community of contributors to GitLab
1. Increase contributions that improve GitLab and the GitLab product
1. Bring results for customers by addressing customer feature and bug requests through contributions
1. Foster diversity, equity and inclusion in our wider community

[Unique Wider Community Contributors per Month](/handbook/marketing/developer-relations/performance-indicators/#unique-wider-community-contributors-per-month)

## Projects and strategies

### Contributor journey

* Onboarding
* Community forks
* Tutorial & documentation
* Tooling & development environments
* GitLab Duo and Ultimate licenses
* gitlab-bot support, triaging and reviews
* Issue discovery
  
### Contribution value

* Gamified leaderboard and contributor leveling system 
* Swag shop rewards
* GitLab Forest & tree planting
* Notable Contributors awards
* GitLab Core Team

### Contributor growth

* External outreach & events
* Partnerships & sponsorships
* Conferences & roadshows
* GitLab Hackathons
* Community Discord
* Outreach return contributor campaigns
* Diversity, equity, and inclusion practices
* Community office hours
* Pairing sessions

## FY26 strategy

* Improve the user experience start to finish from landing on the platform to onboarding to earning rewards for contributions
* Increase collaboration with Product and Engineering teams to generate quality issues for contributors
* Delegate more outside support on contributor growth & external outreach initiatives from Developer Relations & Marketing (assessment: we spend too much time on “community growth” outside GitLab and not enough on “contributor success” within GitLab)
