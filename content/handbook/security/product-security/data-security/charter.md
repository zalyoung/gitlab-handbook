## Mission Statement

"Move efficiently, and fix things."

GitLab's evolution is the result of a series of choices made throughout our development. We are fortunate that GitLab's engineering and infrastructure teams, past and present, have been excellent stewards of GitLab's security and the data entrusted to us by our customers; their work at all levels is to be admired. At our size, however, there are two separate---but related---areas of work that have not been directly included in a team's charter:

* A focus on complex technical problems within security that intersect people, process, and technology;
* Remediation of forgotten security debt.

The Data Security team's continuing mission is to explore GitLab's products in design and implementation, seek out new intersectional security questions and areas of forgotten security debt, and move boldly to prevent and remediate issues before they cause customer impact or slow down engineering velocity.

Our sibling teams, [Infrastructure Security](/handbook/security/product-security/infrastructure-security) and [Security Logging](/handbook/security/product-security/security-logging), focus on key areas within Product Security where dedicated personnel are required to maintain focus on known, key security efforts supporting our product. DataSec, by way of contrast, focuses on *unknown and unexpected* security efforts that are needed to decrease friction on the path to delivering results for customers. We do not gate any functions in the [SDLC](https://www.wiz.io/academy/secure-sdlc), but work throughout GitLab, in partnership with every team from Finance and Legal to Infrastructure and Engineering, to increase both the velocity and the potential for transformative impact of GitLab's work.
